# gate-1 — two deltas, one decision each

This is the gate that cannot be automated, and the cheapest place in the whole process to catch a bad decision. No code exists for either of these.

## Delta A — filter the catalogue by price

Lives in [`spec/changes/filter-catalog-by-price/`](../../spec/changes/filter-catalog-by-price/), because that is where a delta awaiting Gate 1 actually sits. Drafted by [`prompts/delta.md`](../../prompts/delta.md) from [issue 003](../../issues/003-filter-catalog-by-price.md), with no guidance beyond the issue.

It is sound, and it does one thing worth copying: it separates **decisions taken** from an **open question for Gate 1**. It picks the things that are not coin flips and says why, then hands up the one that genuinely is a judgement call — whether `max_price=0` is a filter with an empty answer or a mistake to refuse. It even argues against its own choice, citing `REQ-ORD-6` as precedent for refusing zero.

That is what a delta should feel like arriving on your desk. Not "approve this", but "here is what I decided, here is what is yours."

**One flaw in it, found later and worth knowing before you teach this.** The open question is in the right spirit but the wrong file: it sits inside `spec.md`, the text that *becomes* the living spec, where it both specifies `max_price=0` → `200` and asks you to confirm it. The Executor built what the approved text said and then objected — *"a delta shouldn't ship with an open question inside the text that becomes the spec; the answer belongs there, the question doesn't."* It is right. An open question belongs in `proposal.md`, which is written for the person deciding and does not survive the merge. Good instinct, wrong place — and the loop caught it two stations later.

This one gets merged, and Part 1 of the workshop follows it into the build.

## Delta B — cancel an order

In [`delta-b/`](delta-b/). It reads reasonably. Decide whether you would merge it before you open [`delta-b/review.md`](delta-b/review.md).

Then read the review and compare it to your own list. Judge the review too — anything it missed is its own finding.

## Provenance

The two deltas differ, and this is the one place in `artifacts/` where something was written by hand.

- **Delta A is real output.** Generated by the delta prompt from the issue.
- **Delta B was written by hand, with its flaws planted on purpose.** An exercise needs a known answer, and a sound delta makes a poor thing to practise refusing.
- **Both advisory reviews are real runs** of [`prompts/spec-review.md`](../../prompts/spec-review.md), which had no idea one delta was constructed.

That last point is the interesting one. The review of delta B found every flaw that was planted and four more nobody planted: an ownership model the system does not have, a timestamp the proposal promises and no requirement delivers, a surface named in one scenario that exists nowhere in the spec, and a response body two people would implement differently. It also noticed that an untestable requirement plus a coverage gate that only checks a requirement is *named* will produce a fake test — which is the same lesson Part 2 opens with, arrived at from the other direction.
